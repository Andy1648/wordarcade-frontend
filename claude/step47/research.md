# STEP 47 research — leaderboards that pull players in

1. **slither.io / agar.io** — the name box sits ON the play button; no account, the board is live in the
   corner the whole match. Feels good because the board is never abstract: you watch your own row climb
   while you play, and the name costs one field. Lesson: claiming must be one field, zero friction, at
   the moment you care (right after you did something).
2. **Duolingo Leagues** — end of every lesson shows "You moved up to #4!" with the row sliding up past the
   names you overtook. Feels good because the rank CHANGE is the reward, not the rank. Lesson: show
   "#12 → #7", animate the delta, and do it at the next natural screen (we use the menu visit).
3. **Kahoot! podium / between-question ranks** — "You're in 3rd place, 120 points behind Ana". Feels good
   because it names the gap and the next target. Lesson: the claim prompt should state the exact place
   ("YOU'D BE #N") — a concrete number beats "join the leaderboard".
4. **Clash Royale / Brawl Stars trophy road** — a red badge on the tab until you look; the badge clears
   only by visiting. Lesson: badge the trophy button after a rank-up until the board is opened, then
   clear it — never nag twice for the same news.
5. **Geometry Dash / old Flash high-score tables (Newgrounds medals)** — empty slots read as invitations
   ("--- 0"). Lesson: an empty board is a recruiting surface: "#N — YOUR NAME HERE?" rows, never fake
   people.

What we take: one-field claim at game over with the exact rank, a delta moment on return, a badge that
clears on visit, and empty slots as invitations.
