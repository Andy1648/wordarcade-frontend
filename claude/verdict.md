# JOB 24 — the honest verdict (chore/verdict, REPORT ONLY)

## Is this game good?

**Yes — as a piece of craft. Not yet — as a thing people can't stop playing.** Those are different
questions and the gap between them is the whole story.

The craft is genuinely impressive and not in doubt: a coherent, consistent Flash-cartoon identity with
no broken screens anywhere I could reach; a menu where *every keystroke earns XP*, so the activation
cost is essentially zero (a real, clever idea most word games would kill for); a deep, honestly-tuned
economy (I ran the 200-hour sim this session — worst dead stretch 8.7h and only in the 170h+ deep
endgame, mode spread a tight 1.55×, momentum never maxes, Key Power paces cleanly); a Category Blitz
**AI judge** that's a real differentiator; a SAT Rush "spell-along" mechanic that's a legitimately novel
vocab test; 638 categories; five modes; accessibility, offline, analytics, error boundaries all wired.
For a solo-built browser title, this is a lot of real, shipped engineering and design taste.

But "well-built" is not "good" in the sense that matters. **Good means a stranger keeps playing.** And
here I have to be harsh.

## What a stranger says after five minutes

Roughly: *"Oh cute, typing earns points. The menu looks great — like a real game. Let me try Word Bomb…
nobody's here. Category Blitz… also empty. OK, solo then. SAT Rush is actually a decent little vocab
quiz. Huh, there's a shop. And rebirth. And a collection. And achievements. And themes. And a rank
ladder. …What am I supposed to be *doing*?"* They leave impressed by the polish and unsure of the point.
They will not tell a friend, because they can't say in one breath what the game *is*.

## What is still weak (the harsh part)

1. **The flagship can't fire.** Word Bomb and Category Blitz are the marketed headline — real-time
   multiplayer word battles — and a new indie word game has empty lobbies. You *know* this: the
   self-ghost feature (JOB 13) exists *because* "the lobby is usually empty and that is the core
   multiplayer problem." So the best-sold feature is the least-experienced one. Most players will only
   ever meet the solo modes and the economy grind, while the front door promises a party that isn't
   there. That's the single most damaging mismatch in the product.

2. **The menu writes a check the gameplay doesn't cash.** The five poster-quality mode cards are the
   best-looking thing in the game — and (per this run's own JOBs 2–3 critique) the *in-game* Word Bomb
   and Category Blitz screens still "look like the old app." The first impression oversells the second.

3. **Too many systems, too little spine.** Eight-ish progression systems compete for a new player's
   attention (levels, wins, rebirth, Key Power, Word Sense, Momentum, collection, achievements, themes,
   streaks, rank ladder). That depth is real, but it reads as an idle-game *skeleton bolted onto* a
   skill game, and it invites the suspicion that the economy is *compensating* for core loops that
   aren't quite fun enough to stand alone. A game should earn its second system by making the first one
   irresistible; this one hands you all of them at once.

4. **Two of the five modes overlap.** CHAIN ("each word starts with the last letter") and FUSE ("use
   the alphabet") are both "type words under one constraint," and neither is the reason anyone stays.
   They pad the mode count without widening the appeal.

## The single biggest thing between it and being genuinely fun

**There is no solo mode whose moment-to-moment typing is so good it justifies everything else.** The
multiplayer is the hook but structurally can't deliver it; the economy is scaffolding. Nothing in the
current buffet gives a lone player the "just one more run" compulsion — the thing that makes Balatro or
Vampire Survivors impossible to close. The words themselves need to become the toy.

## The one change I'd argue for

**Build the run-based "WORD JOKERS" mode (the Balatro steal from JOB 21) and make it the flagship the
menu leads with — not multiplayer.** A single-player roguelite run where, between rounds, you draft
stacking modifiers ("×2 for 7-letter words", "+mult per vowel", "rare words add their length") and watch
your score engine explode. It is the highest-leverage change available because:
- It needs **no other humans** — it works from the first player on day one, unlike the multiplayer.
- It reuses the economy **you already built** — the rarity × combo × lucky multipliers are computed and
  hidden *right now*; this just *surfaces them as collectible, combinable jokers*. Almost no new math.
- It turns "type words fast" into "assemble a broken build," which is the exact loop the game is missing
  and the exact reason people replay modern games.

## What I'd cut to make room

1. **Demote multiplayer from the headline.** Keep Word Bomb / Category Blitz as a "play with friends"
   option, but stop leading with a party that's usually empty. The featured slot goes to the run mode.
2. **Retire one of CHAIN / FUSE** (or merge them into one "constraint" mode). Redirect that design
   attention to the run mode.
3. **Hide most of the economy behind progress.** Do not show a day-one player rebirth *and* rank ladder
   *and* collection *and* achievements *and* themes *and* three upgrade tracks. Reveal 3–4 systems that
   directly feed the core loop; unlock the rest as they play. Depth kept, wall removed.

Net: the game is a beautifully made *arcade of systems* in search of a *reason*. It has every part of a
great game except the one loop that makes you forget you were only going to play for a minute. Build
that loop, lead with it, and cut the empty-lobby promise and the mode padding to give it room. That is
the difference between "impressively made" and "good."
