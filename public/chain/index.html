What is Chain?

Chain is a solo word game built on one rule everybody already knows: your next word must begin with the last letter of your previous word. Type ROCKET and the next word has to start with T. Type TANGLE and you’re handed an E. The run is one unbroken chain, and it lasts exactly as long as you can keep feeding it.

What makes it a game rather than a pub exercise is the pressure. You have one life — a single timeout ends the run, with no second chance to fall back on. And the clock is not constant: the opening word gives you a generous eighteen seconds, and every link you complete shaves that down, closing in on a four-and-a-half second floor. Early on you are browsing your vocabulary. Forty links in, you are reacting.

The other thing the game watches is where you leave the chain. Keep ending your words on the same convenient letter and that letter gets hot — each repeat gives you noticeably less time when it comes round again. It exists to stop one comfortable loop from carrying a whole run, and it means a good chain is one that keeps moving into new letters instead of parking on a favourite.

How to play

  1. You are given an opening letter. Type any word that starts with it — at least three letters.
  2. Whatever letter your word ends on becomes the letter your next word must start on.
  3. Keep going. Each accepted word is a link, and the timer for the next one is a little shorter than the last.
  4. No word may be used twice in a run, and every word has to be a real one from the dictionary.
  5. Run out of time and the chain breaks. That is the run — you only have the one life.

Rules

  • One life. A single timeout ends the run. There is no safety net, which is the whole reason a long chain is worth something.
  • Three letters minimum, no repeats, real words only.
  • The clock shrinks as you go. Eighteen seconds on the first word, sliding toward a floor of four and a half. It never quite reaches the floor, but late in a run it may as well have.
  • Hot letters. Every time you end a word on a given letter, that letter gets hotter and hands you less time when it next comes up. Dumping onto the same ending over and over is the one thing the game actively punishes.
  • The multiplier builds as you chain. It climbs a quarter-step per link and caps at two and a half times. Each word scores on its length times that multiplier, so long words deep into a hot chain are where the points are.
  • Dead ends are real. Some letters have very few common words left once the obvious ones are gone, and the game warns you when the letter you are about to hand yourself is running dry.

Tips

  • Watch the letter you are handing yourself, not the one you are on. The word you type is the easy part; the letter it leaves behind is the actual decision. Ending on a vowel is almost always kinder than ending on Y.
  • Spread your endings out. Because repeated endings get hot, three different comfortable letters will carry you much further than one letter used three times.
  • Long words are worth more, but only if you have them ready. The multiplier scales with length, and late in a run the clock will not wait while you hunt for something impressive.
  • Heed the dead-end warning. If the game says a letter is running low, spend a word getting off it rather than discovering the problem with four seconds left.

FAQ

Is Chain free?

Yes — completely free, in the browser, no account needed. Open it and play.

Do I really only get one life?

Yes. One timeout ends the run. It is the shortest, sharpest mode on the site for exactly that reason — a run is a single held breath.

Why did that letter give me less time than last time?

Because you have ended words on it before. Repeated endings heat a letter up and shorten its clock, so the chain has to keep finding new ground.

Can I play Chain on my phone?

Yes. It is built for a phone keyboard as much as a desktop one — one input, one word at a time.
